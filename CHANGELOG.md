# Release history

Latest first. Students see course changes under **Course updates** in each guide's Guide tools, and website changes on the **Website updates** page. Older entries record the behaviour at the time; later releases supersede them.

## Unreleased — A darker dark mode

- **The dark page is near-black now** (`#0E0E10`, was the graphite `#262629`), with cards and pills `#1A1A1D` and hairlines `#2C2C31`; text, TMU Blue and the deadline colours are unchanged and read better still (body text 17.5:1). The page is now darker than the dark panels' ink, so the windows onto the artwork, the signal field and the badge read as raised graphite panels instead of recesses. Safari's flat colour past a light end on iPhone and iPad is the new page under the dark grain (`#0F0F11`), and the offline page follows. Light mode is unchanged.

## Unreleased — Dark mode

- **The website follows the device's light or dark setting.** There is no switch: `prefers-color-scheme: dark` turns the page dark, and a device that changes scheme during a visit (at sunset, say) changes the page at once. Light mode is unchanged: every element's computed colours, the root tokens and screenshots of every page at 390 and 1440 px (and as an iPhone) match the previous build exactly.
- **Only the page, and what sits on it, changes.** The paper becomes graphite (`#262629`), lighter than the artwork behind it, so the windows onto the artwork still read as recesses; text is `#F4F4F5`, cards and pills `#2F2F33`, muted text `#A1A1AA`, hairlines `#3F3F46`. TMU Blue is lifted to `#66B0FF` (links, the selected tab and filters, focus rings) and the deadline colours to `#FBBF24`, `#F87171` and `#4ADE80`. Every dark panel keeps its colours: the menu and Search, the footer (its back-to-top button stays a white disc), the course cards, the MENU pill, the badges, the signal field and the background artwork. The pointer trail needs nothing new: its difference blend shows it light on the dark page.
- **The film grain** is the same texture with its grey inverted at half its alpha (`public/other/grain-dark.png`), so it shows as fine light grain on the graphite and lifts the panel ink by about one level, as the light grain does, so the ink Safari paints past a dark end still meets the page without a line. On iPhone and iPad the flat colour past a light end is the dark page as seen through it (`#27272A`), and `theme-color` follows: one meta per scheme, both kept current.
- **How.** `--black` stays the dark panels' ink in both schemes, as the Safari edge rules require; a new `--text` takes its place wherever it was text on the page or a light card, and `--panelText` replaces `--white` wherever that was text on a dark panel. Both have the old light values. `colors.scss` redefines the page's tokens in `@media screen and (prefers-color-scheme: dark)`, mirroring `DARK` in `lib/palette.mjs`; printing stays light. The course pages no longer write the default palette inline (it would have held the light colours in dark mode). Schedule-X's event colours are the palette's variables, and its grid lines, Today button and pressed greys follow the page. The Guide tools scrim is deeper in dark mode. The edge of the page beside an open Menu or Search is `#71717A` on wider screens; on phones, where that page dims to 5%, it keeps the paper's light edge. A device that switches scheme while Menu or Search is open gets the page back when the panel closes (the shell is rewound before it is rebuilt). `color-scheme` is now `light dark`, so form controls and scrollbars follow, and Chrome's automatic dark theme leaves the site alone. The offline page and the forwarding pages follow the scheme too.
- `tests/dark-mode.test.mjs` checks the dark map against `DARK`, that no dark-panel colour changes, AA contrast for every dark pair, that no text uses the panel ink or the page colour, the metas, and the grain maths for both under-grain colours. `scripts/check-dark-mode.mjs` (Pages CI) loads every page in Chromium as a dark phone and desktop and checks the tokens, theme-color, the MENU label and the text contrast of everything visible, opens Menu, Search, Guide tools and an assignment, checks Safari's document layout as an iPhone, switches scheme mid-visit and back, ending exactly as it started, and switches scheme with Menu open. `check-pages-site.mjs` now expects `light dark` and one theme-color per scheme.

## Unreleased — Pages chosen in Menu or Search grow out of the menu's card

- **No grey wash or white flash when a page is chosen in Menu or Search.** The page change started its paper fade while the panel's 0.92 s close was still playing, so the old page, the dark panel and the new page mixed into a washed grey for most of a second, and on iPhone the paper flashed white before the new page rose out of it. Now the panel's rows fade where they stand from the tap (`fadePanelRows`), the old page changes behind them while it is still the dim card the panel made of it, and the new page grows back out of that card to full size as the dark recedes (`growPageBack`, 0.7 s), the way closing the menu brings a page back. The header and the 3D layer come in as it settles. The animations are Web Animations, which the browser plays on its compositor while the new page sets up. Every screen uses this hand-off; computers no longer wait for the whole menu close before Giats's card slide, and the old card fades out beside the panel instead. With Pause motion the new page is simply there, as before. If the page cannot be opened, the rows come back.
- **iPhone and iPad.** The card is refitted to the new page's first screen before it grows, and refitted again if the page's height changes while it is shrunk. The card's reach under Safari's bars and the colour past the page's ends come back with it on the card's own curve (`--panel-cover`, `--panel-ink`; `cubicBezier` gives GSAP the CSS curve), and the navbar's shell rebuild for the new page no longer resets them in between.
- **No pale box before a course badge appears.** Each course window showed its HTML card (white, with the course code) until the 3D badge drew, then swapped to the badge at once. This was most visible on Courses, the first time the badge physics loads. Where WebGL works, the card now waits up to 2.5 s for the badge, so the window stays dark and the lanyard drops into it; the badge's ribbon and clip no longer draw before its face, and the Courses card opens its window only once the badge has drawn. Without WebGL, or once the 3D layer fails, the card shows at once, as before.
- **Android.** A page chosen in the menu could leave the pointer fluid hidden for the rest of the visit: the menu's shell was stopped before it put the fluid back. The hand-off restores it.
- **Interrupted hand-offs end cleanly.** Back (toolbar, gesture or swipe), a second pick before the first page has loaded, or the menu reopened while a page loads leaves no hidden pointer fluid or 3D layer, no frozen card waiting for the 1.5 s watchdog, and no rows flashing back or missing. On iPhone and iPad a pick made while the menu was still opening starts Safari's edges from where the shell was, and refitting the card no longer rewrites its transform from GSAP's cache (`fitShrunkPage` sets the origin directly).
- `tests/menu-handoff.test.mjs` runs `growPageBack`, `fadePanelRows` and `cubicBezier` with real GSAP. `scripts/check-route-filter-motion.mjs` (iPhone, Menu → course) now requires the arriving course only to grow, to be one clipped card while it is smaller than the screen, to end full width, and never to show the menu's rows or the old page once it is in.

## Unreleased — Trial: a browser-scrolled page (opt-in)

- **Opening the site with `?scroll=fast` turns on a trial of faster scrolling in this browser; `?scroll=classic` turns it off.** Nothing changes for anyone who does not use the link, and nothing on screen looks different. iPhone and iPad already scroll this way and are not affected.
- **What it changes.** Outside iPhone and iPad, the page's `main` box is `overflow: hidden` and Lenis moves it by writing its scroll position every frame, so the browser repaints the whole visible page for each frame of a scroll. In the trial, `main` is a real scroller while Lenis is running, so the browser slides the already painted page instead. Lenis still handles every wheel and touch as before, with the same easing.
- **What it keeps.** The reason `main` was `overflow: hidden` (a pan must never become the browser's own panning, which would take the gesture from Lenis and the fluid trail) is kept with `touch-action: pinch-zoom`, and a two-finger gesture locks `main` while it lasts (wherever the fingers land, the header included), so a pinch still zooms. The menu, Guide tools, the loader and page changes still lock the page. Input that scrolls nothing today still scrolls nothing: a scroll key Lenis does not take locks `main` for that key press, as it is locked today, so a focused note, date field, text field or button still does what the key does there and nothing else moves (the page positions match today's key by key); a wheel over the Schedule calendar that the calendar cannot take, and middle-click autoscroll, are cancelled (a long note still autoscrolls itself). Arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Space still glide through Lenis. With motion paused, the page scrolls natively, as it already does.
- **Differences to expect.** Find in page now moves the page to a match (Chromium skips a box the user cannot scroll), and screen readers can scroll the page, as they already can with motion paused; Lenis follows both. On some Windows screens at 100–125% scaling, Chrome keeps repainting a scroller whose background is not opaque, to keep subpixel text, so the trial may make little difference there. Keyboard focus and links to a section move the page as before.
- `scripts/check-scroll-trial.mjs` checks both modes in Chromium (CI runs it after the touch check), `QA_SCROLL_TRIAL=1` runs `check-touch-fluid.mjs` with the trial on, and `tests/scroll-trial.test.mjs` covers the switch and the input guards.
- Measured on the same build in Chromium (fast repeated phone swipes at a quarter of the CPU speed, the GPU emulated in software): the Desk went from 19 to 23 frames per second, a course page from 31 to 35 and Courses from 19 to 23, and frames slower than 33 ms fell from 72% to 58%, 30% to 22% and 78% to 60%. In a minimal Chromium test, the same scroll repainted the page 478 times with `overflow: hidden` and 34 times as a real scroller.

## Unreleased — Smooth motion at 120 Hz

- **The motion looks the same at 120 Hz as at 60 Hz, only smoother.** Browsers draw at the screen's refresh rate (Chrome, Edge and Firefox at 120 Hz and above; Safari at up to 60 fps unless its "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps" feature flag is off). Several effects were tuned per drawn frame, so on fast screens they ran too fast or too strong. Each now applies its 60 Hz constants for the share of a 60 Hz frame it covers (`src/utils/frameStep.mjs`). Frames from about 70 Hz down count as one whole step, so 60 Hz screens keep the original motion bit for bit.
- **Pointer trail.** The fluid stepped 0.016 s, faded and added dye once per drawn frame, so at 120 Hz the trail moved twice as fast and faded in about half the time. The celebration bloom keeps its full dye. The effect also showed a buffer captured when it was created while the simulation swaps buffers on every pass, so a fading trail refreshed only every other frame (30 Hz on a 60 Hz screen); it now shows the current one.
- **Badges.** The yaw correction runs once per physics step instead of once per drawn frame, so the card no longer turns back to face front faster at 120 Hz. It is skipped while the card sleeps, where it used to build up and spin the card on the next scroll. The strap follows the joints' interpolated poses, like the card, instead of their 60 Hz physics positions, and its catch-up is per 60 Hz frame.
- **Touch scrolling and the Desk's dot field.** A fling travels as far at 120 Hz, and a slow flick starts one (Lenis's 5 px release gate is measured per 60 Hz frame). The dot field follows the pointer at the 60 Hz speed.
- **Badge sharpness.** drei's PerformanceMonitor counted every incline as a flip-flop, so a smooth device fell back to pixel ratio 1 about 10 seconds after load. Only real changes count now, and on screens above 100 Hz the canvas steps down below 90 fps instead of 60.
- **Less work per frame.** The pointer trail's canvas draws only while the trail moves or fades (it rests 10 s after the last input). Its shader materials are built once instead of on every resize frame of a page change, passes no longer force a program check, and its composer drops multisampling and the depth buffer for its empty scene. The shared canvas no longer redraws an empty scene on pages without a badge. The strap is rebuilt only when it moves, a card draws in 2 batches instead of 6, and the dot field computes each pulse once per frame. Drawn output is unchanged.
- **Badges while scrolling fast.** Each badge window now reads the page's layout once per frame, before any badge writes, and the directory cards' clip-path writes come after every badge has drawn; before, the writes fell between other badges' reads, so the browser recomputed styles several times a frame on the Courses page. The clip-path is rewritten only when it changes (the browser keeps it to six significant digits, so comparing against the stored value missed on most scrolling frames). A strap whose window is off screen is rebuilt when it is next drawn, not every frame, and a window crossing into view re-renders only its own lanyard and badge. Drawn output is unchanged.
- A deterministic fake-clock Chromium harness compared every effect at 60 and 120 Hz: the trail, field, fling and badge swing match within a few per cent, against a trail that faded twice as fast, a fling that went half as far and a field that eased twice as fast before. The new `tests/badge-frame-rate.test.mjs` and additions to the fluid, touch and badge tests check the per-frame scaling.

## Unreleased — Smoother fast scrolling

- **Repeated fast scrolling up and down does less work per frame.** Nothing on screen changes; each fix removes work the page did and then threw away.
- **Pre-footer game.** Each target made its own material, and disposing it on a slice or miss released the shader, so the next target compiled it again on the main thread. The targets now share one material per image, built once.
- **Scrollbar.** Each scroll frame started two new tweens while the old ones kept running (about 18 at once at 60 Hz), all writing the bar's position. The bar keeps one position chase and replaces only its own fade, so the menu's animation of the bar is untouched.
- **Layers.** The Desk's Scroll Down marquee pauses while the masthead is off screen. The header's white clearance band is painted at its own height instead of as a full-height gradient.
- **Crossings.** Appearing titles render once per crossing instead of twice. A course tile already showing its number no longer rewrites the same text every frame for a second each time it scrolls into view, and the Checklist and Deadlines shapes no longer keep a superseded tween running when a fast scroll leaves a section mid-animation.

## Unreleased — Opens without a connection

- After one visit, the Desk, Courses, all three guides, Privacy and Website updates open without a connection. `site/public/sw.js` saves this site's own pages and files in the browser; nothing comes from or goes to another host.
- Pages and their data are always fetched from the network first, so an online visit gets the latest dates; the saved copy is used only when the network fails. Build files, whose names change with their content, are served from the copy, so a repeat visit to a course page skips about fifteen revalidation requests once the browser's ten-minute cache has expired.
- An address that was never saved shows a short "You're offline" page with a link to the Desk instead of the browser's error page.
- `site/scripts/check-offline.mjs` registers the copy, goes offline, opens every course, an assignment, a link between courses and an unsaved address, and checks that a changed page wins over the saved one once back online. It runs in Pages CI. Other browser checks run without the copy.

## Unreleased — Grade calculator in every course

- **Grades** is a new tab in each course guide, between the course's other views and Course info. It opens a grade calculator built from the same weights as Course info → Grading: CEN100's 20 graded items in five groups, and the math courses' lab quizzes, midterm and final.
- Marks go in as a percentage or as a score out of a total (17 / 20; typing "17/20" into the mark field fills both, and Enter moves to the next mark). The calculator shows the current average with its letter and grade points, the share of the course already secured, the best grade still possible and how much is still to be marked, with a 0–100 bar of secured, still possible and no longer possible points. When a low quiz that counts now would be dropped later, it says how much higher keeping the average finishes.
- Marks come first on the page. Phones and short laptop screens keep the average, letter and chosen target in a compact summary above the marks while scrolling; on screens tall and wide enough, the full summary stays beside the marks instead. An impossible entry ("Over 100%", "More than 20") shows on the field at fault once its total is finished, and screen readers hear one summary after typing pauses rather than every keystroke.
- **What do I need?** lists every letter from A+ to D- with the average needed on everything still to be marked, marking those already secured and those out of reach. Choosing one explains it in a sentence (naming the item when only one is left, such as the final exam) and marks it on the bar.
- MTH140 and MTH141 drop the lowest lab quiz, as their quiz policies state (best 3 of 4 and best 4 of 5). A quiz is marked Dropped only once more quizzes are entered than count, and a quiz still to come can replace a low one.
- Letters follow TMU's undergraduate scale (Senate Policy 170(a), formerly Policy 46), including its rule that the final percentage is rounded to a whole number, half up, before it becomes a letter.
